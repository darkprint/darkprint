On the sheetcyanotype pole

One ink on the sheet, for the blueprint sections and anywhere the mark sits on blueprint-deep. The kind colours drop out: on the cyanotype pole the drawing is one ink, which is what the rest of the site already does there.

The idea in your reference is not just right, it is already canon: components/home/lifecycle/Folder.tsx draws a folder in --color-blueprint-line that opens to show blueprint.dot, cards/*.yaml and the two documents. “A blueprint is a folder of text” is the site’s own sentence.

The dotted column on the left is kept from your drawing. It reads as sheet perforation, which is the one piece of technical-drawing vocabulary the logo can carry for free.

What the reference had to lose

The boxes. GraphThumbnail replaced rounded rectangles with lit discs because the site was saying what a node is in two languages one click apart, and flow.test.ts now fails on any rect element under components/graph/. A mark built from boxes reinstates the register the repo has a test against.

The neon. Button.tsx rejected a zero-offset coloured halo in as many words: it says the element is emitting light, which is decoration. The luminous register here is one halo per disc at 12%, never an outline around everything.

The cyan body. Cyan is the interactive semantic. A mark entirely in it says click me. The folder is blueprint-line; only the discs inside spend an accent.

The wordmark keeps the Dark/Print split exactly as SiteHeader writes it, and the letterforms are the site’s own: generate-wordmark-paths.ts traces the hero’s morphing wordmark out of Space Grotesk SemiBold 600 — the same family and weight the header sets in live type — so the mark sits beside one set of letterforms, drawn as outlines in the hero and as text everywhere else. It sits left of the wordmark and never replaces it.

Reduction
64 32 24 16 · favicon

The mark sheds nodes rather than shrinking them: three discs and two edges at 64, two discs and one edge from 32 down, and the tab and back plate go at 16. Three 3-unit cores in a 64 box land near 1.5px at 32, which is under the site’s own legibility floor — so the third node leaves rather than becoming a smudge. Stroke weight rises as the size falls, so the folder holds its shape instead of thinning into a hairline. The reference’s ten interior elements have no version of this — at 24px they become one blue smudge.

Where the essay lands

Decision 3 took “Towards a Dark Factory” out of the Learn sequence and it needed a home. The doors band is it: a reader who has just been asked to choose between two paths, and is not sure either applies to them, is exactly who that essay is written for.

Still open

The landing still never shows a real blueprint. Nine parsed, scored, downloadable bundles, and a cold visitor meets only illustrations. A short strip of real tiles before the doors is the strongest argument the site has and the one it withholds — say the word and I will draw it.
