## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment.** This site is two products wearing one skin, and only one of them is authored.

The figure language could not be lifted anywhere else. `FlowAbsence` is a first-class drawing primitive **for an edge that does not exist**, with its label permanently on rather than hover-revealed — almost no design system can draw what isn't there, and this one leads with it. `ScoreRadar` **refuses to plot autonomy**, because a radial axis makes distance-from-centre mean "more" and a human-in-the-loop graph is not a deficient graph: an ethical position expressed as geometry. `.route-box` separates wayfinding from "not built" **by shape rather than hue**, because the palette is already semantic. The cyanotype pole is real, not dark-mode-with-blue-accents.

The chrome and the two registry surfaces are category-default and would survive a find-and-replace onto npm or Hugging Face unchanged. `GalleryBrowser` and `NodeBrowser` are the same stock catalog template twice: search box with a `/` glyph, N dropdowns, chip row, `{n} of {m}`, "Clear filters", 3-column grid, centred empty state.

**Verdict: authored where it illustrates, generic where it operates — and the two never meet.** A visitor walks five bespoke cinematic beats and lands on a form. That register break *is* the labyrinth the author keeps describing.

**Deterministic scan.** 1 finding in `app/`, 0 in `components/`: `codex-grid-background` at `app/globals.css:102`. Browser overlay on 5 routes: `/` 33, `/blueprints/starter-software-factory` **131**, `/nodes/code-builder` 36, `/ontology` 47, `/concepts` 34.

Where the two agree: the **skipped heading** (B's `skipped-heading`; A's measured `H1 → H3 → H3 → H2`; verified independently against the live DOM), **undersized text** (B: 59 sub-11px instances on blueprint detail; A: radar labels at ~8.7px effective), **contrast failures** (B: 3.3:1, 4.3:1, 2.5:1 white-on-colour badges; A: `GalleryBrowser` placeholder at 1.83:1), and **line length** (B: 18 hits on blueprint detail; A: 13 passages over 40 words in `steps.tsx`).

B caught what A missed: `cramped-padding` ×8 on one repeated ontology pill, `nested-cards` ×10 on blueprint detail and ×4 on `/concepts`, `all-caps-body` on `/concepts`, `clipped-overflow-container` ×4.

## Overall Impression

The landing page already solves the problem the rest of the site has. Five beats, 322 words total, each section 0.89–1.14 viewport heights, one figure and one sentence each — and it is enforced structurally, because `beats.test.ts` fails the build on a YAML block or a table inside a beat. That is exactly the "one concept at a time" the author has asked for three times, achieved, in one place, and then abandoned at the gallery door.

The single biggest opportunity is not more copy-cutting. It is that **the shelf does not look like the product.** Nine blueprints are rendered as text tiles behind 36 controls, on a site whose whole strength is drawing graphs.

## What's Working

1. **`FlowAbsence` — a primitive for the edge that isn't there.** Label stays on without hover, so the one thing the site is built on is readable by someone who never points at anything. It works because it makes the thesis land in a single figure — which is exactly why `SectionBlueprint` needs only one sentence of copy.
2. **The landing's pacing discipline.** 322 words across 5 sections, each within one screen at 1440×810. It works because the constraint is enforced by a test rather than by intention. This is the template the rest of the site should be measured against.
3. **Refusing to plot autonomy on the radar.** Five spokes, not six, so the chart's geometry *cannot* express a claim the product believes is false. The belief is enforced by the drawing, not by a caption.

## Priority Issues

### [P0] The registry surfaces are a different product from the landing — component organization

**What.** `/blueprints`: **36 interactive controls before the first of 9 blueprints**, 30 of them tag pills. `/nodes`: **165 controls, 53 ungrouped sibling cards, 53 sibling `<h2>`s, no pagination**, 18.51 viewport heights at 390px. The `/blueprints` lead spends 51 words defining "blueprint" on the surface where a convinced visitor came to browse, duplicating `/what-a-blueprint-is`, which is its own nav item.

**Why it matters.** This is the Operate surface and the stated goal is downloads. Thirty tag pills for nine blueprints means the average tag returns fewer than one result. A visitor arriving from the landing's argument hits faceting sized for thousands of items.

**Fix.** Cut the gallery bar to search + category + sort. Move tags behind a `<details>` — the pattern this codebase already uses well — or surface only the six highest-count tags. Group `/nodes` by node type using the facet that already exists, with sticky group headings, turning 53 cards into ~6 chunks. Replace the `/blueprints` lead with the one job it has: name the shelf, don't define the noun.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable layout`

### [P0] The "nothing runs here" nagging is still everywhere — copywriting

**What.** ~30 visible instances remain after the `/concepts` cleanup. `DownloadPanel.tsx` carries **three in one panel**; `ForkAction.tsx` **two more** on the adjacent button; `/build` step 7 has **five on one screen**; `/install` has **four plus two `ComingSoonBadge`s on a 46-line page**; `/spec/scoring` has five; `SiteFooter.tsx:152` prints "a concept build · real archive · seeded community" on **every page**.

**Why it matters.** Repetition converts honesty into anxiety. A reader clicks **"Fork blueprint"** — outline button, primary position, beside Download — and is told three times it doesn't happen, then redirected to the button next to it. Five disclaimers cluster around the site's actual conversion moment.

**Ledger constraint, which changes the fix.** `components/site/honesty.test.ts` pins 11 claims. Two are on `/install` (the lead sentence and `metadata.description`) and one is the `/spec` telemetry sentence. Those are **not** removable without deleting the ledger entry in the same commit. Everything in `DownloadPanel`, `ForkAction` and the footer strapline is unpinned and free.

**Fix.** One rule: a "not built" marker appears only as a `ComingSoonBadge` on a control the reader can see and is about to touch — never as prose, never twice on a screen. Keep the `runs on your machine` chip (a fact about the artefact, not an apology). Target ≤1 per screen, and invert `honesty.test.ts` so it enforces a **ceiling** as well as its floor.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`

### [P1] Internal spec citations render to users, including to screen readers — copywriting

**What.** `components/panes/model.ts:64-92` carries `ref: "doc 1 §3.1"` … `"§3.5"`, printed visibly at `SkeletonPane.tsx:215` and injected into the accessible name at `SkeletonPane.tsx:204`: `aria-label={`${block.label}, ${block.ref}`}`. A screen-reader user hears **"Identity, doc 1 §3.1"** on five consecutive groups. Leaks on 6 routes.

**Why it matters.** It cites a document the reader cannot open, and it is the clearest possible signal that internal notes escaped into the product.

**Fix.** Drop `ref` from the visible label and the `aria-label`. Keep it as a source comment.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`

### [P1] Leads and field descriptions run to paragraph length — copywriting

**What.** `app/upload/page.tsx:69` is an **89-word** lead that defines the page and disclaims four axes and publishing in one breath. `components/build/steps.tsx` has **13 passages over 40 words**, longest 87; the cap slider alone carries **~240 words**. `app/reading-the-radar/page.tsx:123-131` gives autonomy **71 words** and the other five axes one line each — asymmetric on the page whose whole job is teaching the chart.

**Why it matters.** A field description should say what the field is for and stop. These say what it is for, then what it is not for, then why, then what changes when you move it, then what doesn't.

**Fix.** Cap every `lead` at 25 words answering one question: what is this page for. Everything past sentence one in the `/upload` lead belongs in the `<details>` the page already has. For the cap slider: one label, one reading, one disclosure.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable distill`

### [P2] Heading order, contrast, and sub-legible type

**What.** Blueprint detail runs `H1 → H3 → H3 → H2`: "The graph" and "The card skeleton" are H3s before the first H2 (`GraphPane.tsx:100`, `SkeletonPane.tsx:150`, `SourcePane.tsx:176`). `/upload` has **no `h1`**. `GalleryBrowser.tsx:253` uses `placeholder:text-faint` at **1.83:1**, while its twin `NodeBrowser.tsx:232` uses `text-dim` at 5.43:1 and carries a comment explaining exactly why — the fix was written, reasoned, and never applied to the sibling. `ScoreRadar` labels compute to **~8.7px** effective. Four badge contrast failures at 3.3:1, 4.3:1 and 2.5:1.

**Fix.** Promote the pane headings to H2; give `/upload` an `h1`; copy `text-dim` onto the gallery placeholder; raise radar labels to ~14 viewBox units; darken or outline the badge backgrounds.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable audit`

## Questions to Consider

1. **A blueprint is "a pattern to achieve a given goal" — so why can a visitor filter by six facets and not by goal?** Nobody arrives wanting "a closed-loop blueprint covering debugging." They arrive wanting "something that reviews my PRs." Every facet describes the artefact's *structure*; none describes its *purpose*.
2. **Your best figure draws an edge that isn't there. What would the shelf look like if it were drawn instead of listed?** `GraphThumbnail` already exists. Why does the one surface built to sell blueprints render them as text tiles?
3. **Every "not built yet" is an apology for a promise the site chose to make.** What does the site actually lose if it stops promising — no fork button, no upload panel — until they exist?
4. **If a reader must be taught before they can browse, is the vocabulary wrong or is the browse surface wrong?** Eleven explanatory routes for nine blueprints suggests the registry is failing to carry its own meaning and the docs are compensating.
5. **Composition is what makes blueprints more than templates, and it is represented by a static `⋈` glyph.** What would it take to show one real composition as a single figure — two published graphs, one wired into the other?
